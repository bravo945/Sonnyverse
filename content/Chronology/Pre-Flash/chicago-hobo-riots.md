# Chicago Hobo Riots
> ~2036 - ~2037

The Chicago Hobo Riots were a period of city-wide unrest in regards to the government's tearing of homeless shanty-development throughout the [[chicago-underground|Chicago Underground]].

# "Super-Camps"
Over the former half of the 2030's, homeless communities began to form throughout the Chicago underground in response to a harsh local market, succumbing to AI and manufacturing automation, aswell as the city's lack of extensive support for the poverty line. Such a hostile economic climate pushed populations of various class, family, age, race, and gender, all onto city streets, overflowing the congregation of government-designated "tent cities" throughout former parks and urban-recreational spaces. Because policy for handling this growing epidemic was utterly inefficient, the dangers and lack of proper rationing within the tent cities led people toward the more historic congregation underground.

Migration into these tunnels would see a revival of the Chicago Underground, forming small shanty towns that vaguely resembled scrap-towns of the post-flash era. Media coverage of the shanty-towns would grow with population, with local and national eventually interpreting coverage, coining their name of "Super Camps" and attempting to raise concern and highlighting extensive drug-use within the camps. Shortly after its coverage in media, the police force would start ramping up efforts to dissipate and disband super-camps, giving camps a 1-day notice before rushing in and arresting all residents, who would usually face charges or short jail time.

Attention over Chicago's Underground would only gain traction with the release of an immediately famous documentary, titled "Super Camps: the future of AI Poverty", which covered a team of journalists touring and interviewing the many super-camps downtown. Attempts to sue the film by different companies claiming the properties the super-camps sat within would make global headlines, further inducing controversy and online protest.

# Riots and Protests
The resulting unrest in Chicago from the Fulton Market Raids would become the worst riots in the city's history, locking down the Fulton district for nearly 4 months, while holding curfew and national guard posts throughout city streets for a designated year. Protests of homeless mistreatment served as backdrop-thesis for other Anti-AI initiatives, encouraging assault of all kinds onto corporate property. One event defining such movements were the cyber-attacks that replaced the web-pages of popular chatbot services with a single hastag; "#FreeFulton". This tag would polarize not only the political landscape of the United States, but the entire topic of AI usage globally, representing society's failure to keep citizens safe against the economic threat of artificial intelligence.
