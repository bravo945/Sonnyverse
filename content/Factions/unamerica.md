# UNAMERICA
![[unamerica-overview]]

UNAMERICA is a notorious anarchist movement rooted in the [[chicago-underground|Chicago Underground]], known for their conscious brutality and opposition to absolutely every faction. The radical philosophy of UNAMERICA is the only order given to followers, being that all order is illegitimate and should be collapsed by the people of the remaining world.

# Philosophy
The philosophy of UNAMERICA, though odd, is the cornerstone of the entire project; a decentralized call to disorder that puts into perspective the trauma and radicalization brought forth by the Flash.
# History
## Pre-Flash
### Social Media
UNAMERICA as a movement claims its roots in the Pre-Flash social media sphere, starting with online "echo chambers" congregating in response to the pressures of [[world-war-iii|World War 3]] & the United States draft, the [[big-boom|Big Boom]], and its impacts on the working class. Spear-headed by anarchists, aswell as radical re-interpretations of the Marxist doctrine, the general thesis of this early movement saw the introduction of AI as the last frontier of centralized capital and an existential threat to the working class. These communities fronted a variety of efforts to dissuade big-boom corporations from approaching projects throughout cities in America, from general trashing of technology to formulated protests and riots.

The idea of being an "Unamerican" initially represented a more patriotic "disdain" for the state of the nation, being a compatible term for surrounding movements like the [[new-sons-of-liberty|New Sons of Liberty]]. However, Unamerica in application would hold its inspiration directly from former anarchist activism, aswell as famously efficient crowds of protesters fighting similar circumstances around the world. Because of its anarchist roots, Unamerica as a movement would eventually develop its philosophy further from other parties, opting for a general disdain of order, and a "mocking" of the federal state. This narrative would naturally form groups of preppers, isolationists, and accelerationists across the country, networking online in various endeavours.
### [[chicago-hobo-riots|Chicago Hobo Riots]]
The Chicago Hobo Riots would grow the Unamericans in both numbers, influence, and notoriety, with many riots throwing their name into newsrooms across the country. The "Unamerican" name would come to be both a "boogieman" for household media, aswell as a symbol of brotherhood and relatable sentiment for the fatigued and homeless throughout the Chicago area.
### Minneapolis Anarchy Zone
For a very brief couple of weeks, rioters carrying the unamerican flag in Minneapolis managed to evacuate the city hall, forming a small legal exclusion zone of "Organized Disorder (to quote media sources)". Though attempts to create a community "peace-keeping" system of volunteer security, the chaos of a city-wide riot would naturally break efforts under sudden altercations and pressure from police-profiling technology, which noted the faces of armed "unofficiaries" for future legal action.
## Post-Flash
Unamerica as a decentralized national movement generally died with the flash, apart from the occasional prepper communities that flew the banner. However, Chicago's Unamericans, given their previous struggles and connections underground, would find the symbol a viable ideological tool to survive under. The brutality of nuclear winter would only radicalize the anarchist minds of the underground, each day of survival closer to a world of decentralized idealism.
