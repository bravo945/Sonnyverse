# UNAMERICA
![[unamerica-overview]]

UNAMERICA is a notorious anarchist movement rooted in the [[chicago-underground|Chicago Underground]], known for their conscious brutality and opposition to absolutely every faction. The radical philosophy of UNAMERICA is the only order given to followers, being that all order is illegitimate and should be collapsed by the people of the remaining world.

# Philosophy
The philosophy of UNAMERICA, though odd, is the cornerstone of the entire project; a decentralized call to disorder that puts into perspective the trauma and radicalization brought forth by the Flash.
# History
## Pre-Flash
### Social Media
UNAMERICA as a movement claims its roots in the Pre-Flash social media sphere, starting with online "echo chambers" congregating in response to the pressures of [[world-war-iii|World War 3]] & the United States draft, The [[big-boom|Big Boom]] and its impacts on the working class. Spear-headed by anarchists aswell as radical re-interpretations of the Marxist doctrine, the general thesis of this early movement saw the introduction of AI as the last frontier of centralized capital and an existential threat to the working class. These communities fronted a variety of efforts to dissuade big-boom corporations from approaching projects throughout cities in America, from trashing technology to active protests and riots.

The idea of being an "Unamerican" spawned ironically from more patriotic ideology, stemming from groups like the "[[new-sons-of-liberty|New Sons of Liberty]]", the term being a label one defines themself as to express their disdain for the political and economic landscape of the country. However, as time went on with the movement, Unamerica as a social community would lean further into anarchism, seeing systematic change as a fallacy of public satisfaction. meetings of preppers would start around the country, especially in urban environments and rural areas threatened by fusion centers.
### [[chicago-hobo-riots|Chicago Hobo Riots]]
The Chicago Hobo Riots would grow the Unamericans in both numbers, influence, and notoriety, with many riots throwing their name into newsrooms across the country. The "Unamerican" name would come to be both a "boogieman" for household media, aswell as a symbol of brotherhood and relatable sentiment for the fatigued and homeless throughout the Chicago area.

## Post-Flash
Unamerica as a decentralized national movement generally died with the flash, apart from the occasional prepper communities that flew the banner. However, Chicago's Unamericans, given their previous struggles and connections underground, would find the symbol a viable ideological tool to survive under.
